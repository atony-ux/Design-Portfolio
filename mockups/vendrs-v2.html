MY IMPACT

Two features, one taxonomy holding them together

  • Cut onboarding to four stepsSocial login first, one decision per screen, value before forms.
  • Made discovery spatialA map and list that stay in sync, sorted by how far away the market is.
  • Set the shared primitiveBusiness type, picked once in onboarding, filters the whole product.
THE PROBLEM

The drive was there. The infrastructure wasn't.

Owners ran their shops out of group chats, spreadsheets and Instagram DMs. Markets were heard about through a friend's story, usually too late to apply.

97%want a bigger audience for what they make
80%run the entire shop by themselves
70%want to scale, with no system to do it

They didn't lack ambition. They lacked infrastructure.

The pattern behind all 40+ interview observations
FEATURE 01 · ONBOARDING

Ask for less, and make every answer work twice

Owners abandon tools that demand setup before showing value. The question was how little we could ask and still personalise the product on day one.

✕ Rejected

One long profile form

Complete, but it front-loads the work and reads as paperwork before any value arrives.

✓ Shipped

Four steps, one decision each

Social login opens it, so the highest-friction moment lands before we ask for trust.

FEATURE 02 · EVENTS MAP

Discovery you can see, sorted by what you can reach

Discovery was the loudest pain in research, so I made it spatial: pins for context, a list for scanning, and the closest market in the top slot.

Selecting an event in the list highlights its pin, and the filters come straight from onboarding.

✕ Rejected

A feed of events

Familiar, but it hides the one thing owners judge an event on: can I physically get there.

✓ Shipped

Map and list as one surface

Two views of the same set, synced both ways, sorted by distance and filtered by business type.

WHAT TESTING CHANGED

Two of three changes landed on my features

  • Events Map. Testers read the map and list as separate tools, so I tightened the sync until selecting in one obviously drove the other.
  • Onboarding. I cut the step people called busywork and led with value instead, which lifted completion.
  • Marketplace (teammate's feature). A confusing two-column layout became a scannable horizontal one.
TAKEAWAY

The boring early decision was the one that compounded

Business type looked like a form field. It ended up being the backbone of discovery: onboarding did real work instead of collecting data, and the Events Map inherited personalisation for free. Next, I'd add messaging so owners can mentor each other, and build the customer-facing side to drive foot traffic back to the vendors.
